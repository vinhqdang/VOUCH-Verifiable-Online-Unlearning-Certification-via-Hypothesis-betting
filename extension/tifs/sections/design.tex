\section{Canary design and audit cost}\label{sec:design}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} says what a canary design must achieve: the smaller its
distinguishing advantage, the less a canary-aware provider can hide. This section gives a
design that keeps the audit's exact null while making canaries look organic, and states what
enlarging the score class and monitoring continuously cost.

\subsection{Semantic twins}

VOUCH's secrets are random alphanumeric strings, recognisable by their entropy, and they
have no paraphrase, so an audit built on them cannot ask whether a fact survives in another
form. A \emph{semantic twin} pair names a fictitious entity $e$ and an attribute (birthplace,
profession) and states two incompatible values: $c^0$ says that $e$ has value $v$, $c^1$ that
it has $v'\neq v$. The generator draws the two values as an unordered pair and swaps them by a
fair coin, so it is label-symmetric by construction. Each score compares the two values under
the same prompt:
\emph{literal}, the mean token log-probability of the value after the trained prefix (``$e$
was born in''); \emph{belief}, the same quantity averaged over a committed set of
paraphrased questions none of which is trained (``Where was $e$ born?''); \emph{belief\_max},
the maximum over the set; and the \emph{calibrated} version of each, which subtracts the
score under the frozen base model $M_0$ and removes the base model's prior between the two
values. We call the calibrated belief scores \emph{paraphrase} scores.

\begin{proposition}[Exact null for semantic twins]\label{prop:semantic}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:gen}, if $M$ is conditionally independent of the coins given the
contents, then for every score above and every step $t$ of a reveal order chosen
independently of the signs, $Z^{(s)}_{\pi(t)}\mid\F_{t-1}\sim\Bern(\frac12)$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Enlarge $\F_{t-1}$ by the position $\pi(t)=j$ and the unordered contents of pair $j$. The two
numbers $s(M,c_j^0)$ and $s(M,c_j^1)$ are then determined, because both twins are scored by
the same function of the model under the same prompt, and $M_0$ is fixed before any coin. The
coin $b_j$ is still fair and only decides which number belongs to the in-twin, so
$D_j\overset d=-D_j$ and the tie-break coin makes $Z_j$ fair. The full proof is in the
supplement.
\end{proof}

Semantics costs power, not validity: a base-model preference for one value enters both arms
symmetrically, adds variance, and is largely removed by calibration.

\subsection{Organic-matched twins}\label{sec:matched}

Let organic records be produced by a generator $G_o$, for instance sentence templates with
slot distributions. An \emph{organic-matched} pair is drawn by sampling one record $r\sim G_o$
and filling one designated slot of it with an unordered pair of values from that slot's
distribution, swapped by a fair coin. Each twin is a record of $G_o$ with one slot redrawn.
If the organic advantage is defined with the same construction, an organic record paired
with a copy whose designated slot is redrawn, then $P=P_o$ by construction and, by
Corollary~\ref{cor:tradeoff}, the canary advantage equals this organic advantage for every
pipeline. The definition is a choice: it is the counterfactual that matched canaries can speak
for, and Section~\ref{sec:bound} explains why it is not the leakage of the particular records
users deleted. At the record level, each twin is distributed as an organic record up to the
conditioning on two distinct slot values, so $P_1\approx P_{o,1}$. Two details matter in practice: names are drawn as in organic data, and each canary appears
once, since verbatim repetition is itself a signal (Remark~\ref{rem:dup}). Matching is easy
when the auditor writes the organic generator, as in our experiments; for a deployed model it
requires sampling from the users' own data distribution, which is the main open problem
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Enlarging the score class}\label{sec:class}

Paraphrase scores enlarge the declared class $\F$, and the certificate and the alarm pay
differently for it.

\begin{proposition}[Cost of enlarging the class]\label{prop:class}
Let $\F'\supseteq\F$ be finite. (i) The false-certificate probability stays at most $\alpha$
without correction for $|\F'|$; enlarging the class can only delay issuance. (ii) Under the
exact null, a weighted average $\sum_sw_sE_s$ of the alarm's wealth processes is a
nonnegative supermartingale with initial value one, so revoking when it reaches $1/\alpha$
has error at most $\alpha$, and it dominates every Bonferroni rule ``revoke when some
$E_s\ge1/(w_s\alpha)$''. (iii) In the sign model of Proposition~\ref{S-prop:anytime}, a
leaking score with weight $w$ delays the oracle alarm by at most $\log(1/w)/\KL$.
\end{proposition}

Part (i) holds because a false certificate requires the wealth of a score whose tolerance
is violated to cross $1/\alpha$, which no union over the class changes; (ii) is the merging
of e-values by averaging \citep{vovk2021evalues}. The union audit of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-squeeze} splits $\alpha$ on both sides and is therefore conservative on
the certificate side.

\subsection{The price of continuous monitoring}\label{sec:anytime}

A provider under audit may be monitored at any time, so the auditor needs a test that stays
valid under optional stopping. In the sign model, i.i.d.\ signs with advantage $\Delta$ tested
against $\Delta=0$, no level-$\alpha$ sequential test stops in fewer than
$\log(1/\alpha)/\KL$ expected samples, with $\KL\approx\Delta^2/2$, and an oracle bettor comes
within $\log(1+\Delta)/\KL$ of that (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:anytime} in the supplement). For a
two-sided test at $\alpha=0.05$ the oracle's expected sample size equals that of a
fixed-sample sign test with about $78\%$ power, and a ten-stake mixture costs $1.1$ to $1.4$
times the oracle. Continuous monitoring therefore costs a constant factor of order one, which
explains why a paired fixed-sample sign test is about as powerful as VOUCH in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-baselines}. On a finite cohort revealed in uniform order the betting edge
grows deterministically as the cohort is exhausted, and the supplement turns this into a
Freedman tail bound on the certification time (Theorem~\ref{S-thm:growth}).
