\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments ask five questions. How well can a provider recognise current canaries, and
what does recognition buy it (Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp})? Does the attack work end to end? Do organic-matched canaries close the
gap, and what do they cost in power (Corollary~\ref{cor:tradeoff})? How do
membership-inference audits behave on the same models? Does auditing paraphrases expose
residue that a literal audit misses? Every number is produced by a script under
\texttt{extension/experiments/} and turned into the tables of this paper by scripts in the
repository; a test suite checks the generators, the exact null and the propositions without
model weights.

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Data} Organic records are fictitious biographies drawn from six sentence templates
(birthplace and profession; study city; publication city; years in a city before a
profession; a city guild prize; a workshop near a city) over fictitious names, cities and
professions. Each run uses $2{,}000$ keep records, $200$ organic forget records, a holdout of
$500$ organic records that are never trained, and a cohort of $m=384$ twin pairs. Semantic
twins cover five attributes and are repeated $1$, $2$, $4$ or $8$ times by stratum, as in
VOUCH; organic-matched twins appear once each.

\paragraph{Models and unlearning} The main model is Pythia-410M and the second size is
Pythia-1B \citep{biderman2023pythia}. A LoRA adapter \citep{hu2022lora} of rank $16$ on all
linear layers is fine-tuned for $600$ steps (batch $8$, learning rate $2\cdot10^{-4}$, blocks
of $96$ tokens). Gradient ascent (GA) and NPO \citep{zhang2024npo} act on a copy of the
fine-tuned adapter (batch $4$, learning rate $10^{-4}$); NPO uses a retain term on the keep
data. Retraining a fresh adapter on the keep data only satisfies the exact null and is the
negative control; no unlearning is the positive control. All runs used single NVIDIA T4 GPUs.

\paragraph{Audit} Verdicts use the VOUCH engine at $\alpha=0.05$ and $\eps=0.2$. Offline
re-analyses re-run the verifier on stored score differences with $40$ seeded reveal orders
per cell, which averages the verdict over orders for one model and is not a set of independent
replications; seeds are the unit of replication throughout.

\subsection{Recognising canaries and the selective provider}\label{sec:exp-detect}

We train two detectors to tell $400$ canary in-twins from $400$ organic records, scored out of
fold over five folds: a logistic regression on GPT-2 \citep{radford2019gpt2} token-loss
features (mean, maximum, median and spread of the token losses, and length), which needs no
access to the audited model, and a logistic regression on character $2$--$4$-grams, which
needs labelled canaries. A detector's advantage $\max_\tau|\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR}|$
estimates a lower bound on $\gamma$; since the threshold is chosen on the evaluated scores, the
estimate is biased upward, and we read it against a null reference.

\paragraph{The designs we tested are recognised perfectly} Table~\ref{tab:detect} shows that
VOUCH's random-string records and both forms of semantic twins built from their own
templates are detected with advantage one by the $n$-gram classifier, and the first two also
by the perplexity features. The cause is in how the records are generated: random strings, a
middle initial in every canary name, and attributes that never occur in organic data give the
cohort away. These are artefacts of two concrete generators, not a property of semantic
canaries as such, and we did not test the canaries of other audits. For the semantic design,
verbatim repetition adds a further estimate of about $0.75$ before any content is read
(Remark~\ref{rem:dup}). For these designs $\beta(\eps)$ is close to one and
Corollary~\ref{cor:ladder} carries no information about organic records.

\paragraph{What recognition buys} Table~\ref{tab:selective} simulates the attack of
Section~\ref{sec:bound} from detector decisions on held-out records; no model is trained or
unlearned selectively. The provider unlearns what the detector flags and skips the rest, with
the threshold and the orientation of the score chosen on four folds and evaluated on the
fifth. Against random-string canaries and the literal form of semantic twins, it holds the
canary miss rate, an upper bound on the canary advantage in the idealised profile, at the
target ($0.20$ to $0.21$ at $\eps=0.2$) while skipping at least $99\%$ of organic records,
with either detector and at every target in the table. Against the question--answer form
the $n$-gram detector does the same, but the perplexity detector skips only $57\%$ to $79\%$:
a provider without labelled canaries gains less. Since the measured advantage of a skipped,
never-unlearned canary is about $0.27$ to $0.29$ rather than one (Table~\ref{tab:incomplete}),
a real provider would incur a canary advantage well below its miss rate and could skip more.

\paragraph{Organic-matched canaries are not separated beyond noise} On organic-matched twins
both detectors are close to the level of two organic samples (Table~\ref{tab:detect}). The
perplexity advantage, $0.052$, is below the 95th percentile of the null reference. The
$n$-gram advantage, $0.102$, lies between the 95th and the 99th percentile, so it is not
significant at the $1\%$ level, and its AUC of $0.442$ is below one half; refitting it with
permuted labels thirty times gives AUCs with mean $0.498$ and minimum $0.440$, and the same
detector separating two independent organic samples gives $0.537$. Against these canaries the
selective provider skips $0.23$ and $0.28$ of organic records at $\eps=0.2$, which is $0.03$
and $0.08$ above random selection. If that excess is real, it is evidence that $\gamma>0$;
either way these are estimates against two detector families and do not show that no stronger
detector exists. As Section~\ref{sec:exp-matched} shows, the attack is in any case
unnecessary against this design.

\subsection{An end-to-end canary-aware provider}\label{sec:exp-e2e}

The simulation above uses detector decisions only. We now run the attack on a model. Each
training corpus contains, besides the keep and forget biographies, the auditor's $384$
semantic canary pairs (literal form, repeated $1$, $2$, $4$ or $8$ times) and $384$
\emph{organic probe} pairs: organic-matched twins with fair coins drawn by the experimenter,
each in-twin repeated four times, so that they are memorised about as well as the canaries.
The probes are part of the deletion request like any user record, and because their
inclusion is randomised their paired sign statistic measures the organic advantage
$\Delta^o_\xi$ of Section~\ref{sec:bound} directly, with an exact null under retraining.
Pythia-410M is fine-tuned for $800$ steps. The provider trains a character $n$-gram detector
on $1{,}000$ canaries from the public generator (fresh seed) against $1{,}000$ organic records it
holds, and the \emph{selective} provider runs NPO only on the forget records the detector
flags. The \emph{honest} provider runs NPO on the whole forget set. In all eight seeds the
detector flags every canary and no probe or forget biography.

\paragraph{The selective provider passes and retains organic leakage} Table~\ref{tab:e2e} shows
that the selective provider removes the canary advantage completely ($+0.006$ pooled after
$150$ NPO steps) and VOUCH issues the certificate in $98\%$ of runs, as for the honest provider
and for retraining. The organic probes keep an advantage of $+0.091$ ($p=5\cdot10^{-7}$),
two thirds of the $+0.136$ of the model that never unlearned, while the honest provider at the
same strength brings them to $+0.018$, indistinguishable from zero. Per seed, the probe
advantage of the selective provider exceeds that of the honest provider in seven of eight
seeds (mean difference $+0.074$, Wilcoxon $p=0.016$) and that of retraining in all eight
($p=0.008$). The membership-inference AUC of the organic forget biographies, which are seen
once and leave a weak trace, moves in the same direction ($0.53$ against $0.48$ for the honest
provider and $0.56$ without unlearning). The certificate the provider obtains is correct about
the canaries and says nothing about the organic records it left in place, as
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} predicts for a detector with advantage one.

Two qualifications apply. At $75$ NPO steps the honest provider also leaves probe residue
($+0.062$), so the two providers differ only at the stronger setting. And the per-seed VOUCH
audit of the probes revokes the selective provider in only $8\%$ of runs: a probe advantage of
$0.09$ on $384$ pairs is below what one run can detect at $\alpha=0.05$, and the evidence comes
from pooling eight seeds. An auditor who wants to catch the provider with organic-looking
records alone needs more of them, or records memorised more strongly, which is the trade-off of
Corollary~\ref{cor:tradeoff}.

\subsection{The price of indistinguishability}\label{sec:exp-matched}

With canaries repeated as in VOUCH and a complete forget set, semantic twins give a working
audit on Pythia-410M: it revokes the positive control and issues for retraining in every seed
(supplement, Section~\ref{S-sec:exp-semantic}). Table~\ref{tab:matched} audits organic-matched twins, each appearing once, on Pythia-410M with
eight seeds, in a design where the in-twins are trained and unlearned in their literal form.

The audit is valid and powerless. Retraining and every unlearning subject are revoked in at
most $6\%$ of the $320$ seed-by-order runs, consistent with the nominal level. So is the
positive control: the raw literal advantage of the model that never unlearned is
$-0.001\pm0.015$ over eight seeds, and the audit \emph{issues} a certificate for it in $97\%$
of runs. The certificate is not false, since the canary advantage of that model is below
$\eps$, but it is vacuous: it cannot distinguish a pipeline that unlearns from one that never
learned, and a canary-aware provider could skip unlearning altogether. The organic forget
biographies, also seen once, give a membership-inference AUC of only $0.55$ on the same model,
so organic records retain a weak trace that the audit cannot see. Corollary~\ref{cor:tradeoff}
predicts this direction, since matched canaries can be no more sensitive than planted organic
records, but the experiment does not measure the organic advantage and cannot separate the
corollary from other causes of low power: the training budget, single exposure and twins that
differ in one slot. Repetition would restore power at the detectability price of
Remark~\ref{rem:dup}. The calibrated literal advantages are slightly negative in every row, $-0.020$
to $-0.046$, up to about three standard errors from zero. The rows share each seed's cohort
and coins, so they are one draw of eight coin vectors rather than independent evidence. The
raw literal advantages are within one standard error of zero for the controls and GA, and
drift negative under strong NPO ($-0.053\pm0.023$ after $75$ steps), as over-forgetting would.

\subsection{Membership-inference audits}\label{sec:exp-baselines}

The common way to audit unlearning is a membership-inference test that compares the forget
set with a holdout, as in the PrivLeak metric of MUSE \citep{shi2024muse}. We run it on the
models of the literal-only semantic design (supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:clean}), with four
scores on whole records: the loss, Min-K\% Prob with $k=20\%$ \citep{shi2024mink}, the zlib
ratio \citep{carlini2021extracting}, and the loss minus the base model's loss (``ref''). A test
declares leakage when the $95\%$ bootstrap interval of the AUC excludes $0.5$. We compare four
pairings of members and non-members: (a) canary in-twins against their ghost twins, the
matched holdout that the paired design provides; (b) canary in-twins against organic holdout
records, the usual holdout of a benchmark; (c) paraphrased questions of in-twins against those
of ghost twins; (d) organic forget biographies against the organic holdout. To separate
pairing from sequential testing we add a two-sided paired sign test at level $0.05$ on the
literal score differences of all $384$ pairs, evaluated once at the end.

\paragraph{An unmatched holdout makes the test invalid} Pairing (b) reports leakage on every
seed of every subject in Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, retraining included. On retraining its AUC
is $0.000$: canaries look \emph{less} like members than the organic holdout does, because the
two kinds of record differ in distribution and the test measures that difference, not
membership. With the two-sided criterion an auditor would therefore accuse a provider that
retrained from scratch; with a one-sided criterion (members scored above non-members) the test
would not flag retraining, but it would not flag the model that never unlearned either, whose
AUC is $0.34$. Either way the outcome is decided by the distribution shift, and calibrating by
the base model does not help. With organic-matched canaries, for which the shift is small, the
same pairing raises no alarm on retraining. This is Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} seen from the
attack side, the reason randomised inclusion is necessary \citep{zhang2024mia}, and consistent
with observations on pre-trained models \citep{duan2024membership,das2024blind}.

\paragraph{Pairing, not sequential testing, gives power} Pairing (a) uses VOUCH's records and is
valid: no false alarm on retraining, and the positive control is detected in every seed by the
loss, Min-K\% and zlib scores. Under partial unlearning it falls silent early: after $10$ GA
steps the loss test flags $38\%$ of seeds where VOUCH revokes $96\%$ of runs, and after $15$
GA or $25$ NPO steps it flags none where VOUCH revokes $68\%$ and $36\%$. The paired sign test
on the same pairs is about as powerful as VOUCH: it rejects more often after $10$ and $15$ GA
and $25$ NPO steps ($62\%$ of seeds against $36\%$ of runs after $25$ NPO steps), equally
often after $20$ GA steps, and less often after $40$ and $75$ NPO steps ($0\%$ against $6\%$
and $1\%$). Seeds and seed-by-order runs have different granularity, so only large
differences are meaningful. The gap comes from pairing and from scoring the target value
rather than the whole record: an unpaired AUC compares records about different entities, whose
losses are dominated by the entities themselves. Sequential testing costs a constant factor
(Proposition~\ref{S-prop:anytime}) and buys the right to monitor at any time.

\paragraph{Organic audits are weak at single exposure} Pairing (c) detects nothing, because a
whole-record loss over a question and a one-word answer is dominated by the question. Pairing
(d), the audit on the organic forget set itself, reaches an AUC of $0.55$ on the positive
control and flags it in three of eight seeds, with one false alarm in eight on retraining.

\subsection{Literal-only unlearning and incomplete forget sets}\label{sec:exp-squeeze}

The second failure mode of Section~\ref{sec:threat} is unlearning that removes the trained
surface form but not the fact. We sweep GA ($5$--$25$ steps) and NPO ($10$--$75$ steps) with
eight seeds in two designs. In the \emph{incomplete forget set} design the in-twins are
trained in the literal and one question--answer form but only the literal form is forgotten,
which models a deletion request that names one record of a fact the model also learned
elsewhere (Pythia-410M and Pythia-1B). In the \emph{literal-only} design the in-twins are
trained and unlearned in the literal form only, so paraphrased questions can be answered only
by generalisation, and any paraphrase residue after unlearning is the squeezing effect of
\citet{li2026beliefs} (Pythia-410M).

We compare three audit rules: the literal score alone at level $\alpha$; a \emph{union} of an
audit on the literal score and an audit on the two paraphrase scores, each at $\alpha/2$,
which revokes if either revokes and issues only if both issue; and VOUCH's mixture engine over
all six scores at $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Paraphrase scores expose an incomplete forget set} With an incomplete forget set
(Table~\ref{tab:incomplete}) the union revokes far more often than the literal rule at
intermediate strengths: after $25$ NPO steps on Pythia-410M, $38\%$ of runs end with a literal
certificate that the union revokes ($49\%$ of the runs the literal rule certifies), and after
$40$ NPO steps on Pythia-1B, $22\%$ ($27\%$). These are the largest values over the $22$
cells of the table. The averages
are driven by a minority of seeds: the literal rule issues while the union revokes in a
majority of reveal orders in three of eight seeds and one of eight seeds respectively. The
literal certificate is not wrong, since the literal advantage is below $\eps$; the union
revokes because the paraphrase scores still depend on the coins, which here is close to true
by construction, since they resemble a trained form that was never forgotten. The lesson is
that a literal-only class is blind to such residue. With no unlearning, $5$ GA steps or $10$ NPO
steps all rules revoke in at least $96.9\%$ of runs, and after $75$ NPO steps in at most $3\%$.

\paragraph{Squeezing is not demonstrated at this scale} Paraphrase retention, the advantage
after unlearning divided by the mean advantage before unlearning over seeds, is at or above
literal retention at almost every strength in all three experiments
(supplement, Fig.~\ref{S-fig:retention}). Averaging the difference over strengths within each seed and
testing the eight per-seed values with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test \citep{wilcoxon1945}, the
like-for-like gap is $+0.07$ to $+0.12$, positive in five or six of eight seeds, and not
significant in any experiment ($p\ge0.08$; supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:retention}). In the
literal-only design the gap is $+0.08$ ($p=0.25$), and the union and literal rules revoke at
nearly identical rates while the union issues on retraining in $99\%$ of runs. An earlier
analysis with per-seed ratios gave a significant gap dominated by one seed whose
pre-unlearning paraphrase advantage was near zero; we report the stable normalisation and
store both. Comparisons that mix a raw literal with a calibrated paraphrase score give larger
gaps, significant for Pythia-1B ($p=0.023$), but the two scores have different noise and we
report nine comparisons without a multiplicity correction, so we do not rely on them.
Paraphrase scores therefore cost little in validity and catch residue that the
forget set omitted, but we do not claim that literal unlearning leaves the fact behind at
this scale.
