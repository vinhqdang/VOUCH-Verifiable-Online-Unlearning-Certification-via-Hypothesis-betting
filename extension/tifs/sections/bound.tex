\section{What a canary-aware provider can hide}\label{sec:bound}

\subsection{Leakage profiles}

Fix a pipeline run $\xi$ and write the audit statistic as an average
$\Delta=\frac1m\sum_iT_i$ of per-pair terms $T_i\in[-1,1]$; for VOUCH $T_i=2Z_i-1$. For pair
contents $c$ define the \emph{leakage profile}
\[
h_\xi(c)\;=\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,T_i\;\big|\;C_i=c,\ \xi\,\bigr]\in[-1,1],
\]
the expected contribution of a pair with contents $c$ placed in the cohort, the expectation
being over the coins and the other pairs' contents. A canary-aware provider chooses the
profile through its content-dependent treatment rule. The \emph{canary advantage} of the run
is $\Delta_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P}[h_\xi(c)]$, the expected statistic over fresh cohorts.

Let $P_o$ be a distribution of \emph{organic} pairs: two records of the kind users delete,
with a hypothetical fair coin choosing which was trained. The \emph{organic advantage}
$\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P_o}[h_\xi(c)]$ is the advantage the same run would show if
organic pairs were planted in the same way. It is membership leakage under randomised
inclusion, evaluated on the data users delete, and a counterfactual quantity of the
pipeline, not an observed rate.

\begin{definition}[Distinguishing advantage]\label{def:gamma}
For a class $\mathcal H$ of functions from pair contents to $[0,1]$,
$\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal H}|\mathbb{E}_P\phi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi|$.
For the class of all measurable functions it is the total variation distance $\TV(P,P_o)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The bound}

\begin{theorem}[Special-casing requires detection]\label{thm:detect}
Let $\phi_\xi=(1+h_\xi)/2\in[0,1]$. For every class $\mathcal H$ that contains $\phi_\xi$,
\[
|\Delta^o_\xi-\Delta_\xi|\;=\;2\,|\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi|
\;\le\;2\,\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By linearity, $\Delta_\xi-\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_Ph_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}h_\xi
=2(\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi)$.
\end{proof}

The proof is one line; the content is in the reading. If a run opens a gap $g$ between the
canary and the organic advantage, then $\phi_\xi$ tells canary pairs from organic pairs with
advantage $g/2$. Whatever rule the provider uses to decide what looks like a canary, the gap
it opens is at most twice that rule's advantage, and a provider that cannot recognise
canaries from their contents cannot open a gap at all. VOUCH shows that recognising the
cohort does not threaten the null, since knowing which records are canaries reveals nothing
about which twin the coin admitted \citep{dang2026vouch}. That argument concerns validity on
the cohort and is unaffected. Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} concerns whether the cohort speaks for
organic records, and there recognition is exactly what matters.

The same identity read in the other direction limits the auditor.

\begin{corollary}[Detectability--power trade-off]\label{cor:tradeoff}
For every pipeline run and every $\mathcal H\ni\phi_\xi$,
$|\Delta_\xi|\le|\Delta^o_\xi|+2\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$. In particular, if canaries are
distributed as organic records, $P=P_o$, then $\Delta_\xi=\Delta^o_\xi$ for every pipeline.
\end{corollary}

An audit is powerful when the canary advantage of a non-unlearning pipeline is large. The
corollary says that it can exceed the organic advantage of the same pipeline only by twice
the canaries' detectability. Designs that make canaries easy to memorise, by repetition,
high-entropy secrets or unusual attributes, buy power with detectability; canaries that are
indistinguishable from organic records are exactly as hard to detect in the model as organic
records are. There is no canary design that is both undetectable and more sensitive than the
data it stands in for.

\subsection{A sharp form for one-sided leakage}

The factor two allows over-forgetting, $h_\xi<0$. Call leakage \emph{one-sided} if
$h_\xi(c)\ge0$ for every $c$: no record is forgotten beyond exactness. This holds for an
idealised selective provider that either leaves a record alone or unlearns it exactly. It is
a pointwise assumption that the auditor cannot check, and it need not hold for gradient
ascent, which over-forgets. Let $p,p_o$ be densities of $P,P_o$ with respect to $P+P_o$ and
define the \emph{Neyman--Pearson power}
\[
\beta(\eps)=\sup\bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi\;:\;\varphi:\mathcal C\to[0,1]\text{
measurable},\ \mathbb{E}_P\varphi\le\eps\bigr\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp evasion bound]\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\eps\in(0,1]$, $B^\ast=\{p_o>p\}$ and $a=P(B^\ast)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every run with $h_\xi\in[0,1]$ and $\Delta_\xi\le\eps$ has
$\Delta^o_\xi\le\beta(\eps)\le\min\{1,\eps+\TV(P,P_o)\}$. If $h_\xi\in\mathcal H\subseteq
[0,1]^{\mathcal C}$, then $\Delta^o_\xi\le\Delta_\xi+\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$.
\item[(ii)] $\beta(\eps)$ is attained by a profile $h=\varphi^\ast$, a likelihood-ratio test
of $P_o$ against $P$. Hence no argument that uses only the canary advantage and the
generator can exclude $\Delta^o_\xi=\beta(\eps)$. If $P$ has no atoms on $\{p>0\}$,
$\varphi^\ast$ can be taken to be the indicator of a set of records, which is the profile of
a provider that skips unlearning on that set, provided the skipped records stay favoured.
\item[(iii)] $\beta(\eps)\ge\eps(1+\TV/a)$ for $\eps\le a$ and $\beta(\eps)\ge a+\TV$ for
$\eps\ge a$. In particular $\beta(\eps)=\eps$ if $P=P_o$, and $\beta(\eps)=1$ for every
$\eps>0$ if $P$ and $P_o$ are mutually singular.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\varphi=h_\xi$ is feasible, since $\mathbb{E}_P\varphi=\Delta_\xi\le\eps$, and
$\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi=\Delta^o_\xi$. For $\varphi\in[0,1]$,
$\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi\le\mathbb{E}_P\varphi+\TV$. The class statement is
Definition~\ref{def:gamma}. (ii) The Neyman--Pearson lemma \citep{neyman1933efficient} gives a
possibly randomised likelihood-ratio test attaining the supremum; if $P$ is atomless a
non-randomised test of the same size and power exists. (iii) $\mathbf 1_{B^\ast}$ has
$\mathbb{E}_P=a$ and $\mathbb{E}_{P_o}=a+\TV$; for $\eps\le a$ use $(\eps/a)\mathbf
1_{B^\ast}$. If $P=P_o$, (i) gives $\beta\le\eps$ and $\varphi\equiv\eps$ attains it. If
$P\perp P_o$, then $a=0$ and $P_o(B^\ast)=1$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Every detector is an attack.} Part (ii) turns any canary detector into a provider
strategy. A provider that unlearns the records its detector flags as canary-like and leaves
the rest has a canary advantage of at most the detector's miss rate on canaries, with
equality when the skipped canaries stay favoured, and leaves un-unlearned the fraction of
organic records that the detector correctly passes. Its gain over a provider that skips
records at random, which leaves a fraction equal to the miss rate, is a lower bound on
$\beta(\eps)-\eps$. Conversely every provider strategy is a detector. A certificate needs
the realised advantage strictly below $\eps$ with a margin, so a miss rate of $\eps$ is the
edge of what the audit tolerates, not a point it certainly passes.

\begin{remark}[Repetition is content]\label{rem:dup}
The provider sees the corpus, so a canary repeated verbatim is flagged by the duplicate
detector $\phi(c)=\mathbf 1[c\text{ occurs at least twice}]$, whose advantage is
$P(\text{repeated})-\rho_o$ with $\rho_o$ the share of duplicated organic records. Designs
that repeat canaries to strengthen memorisation pay before any content is examined. The
semantic design of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} repeats three quarters of its twins, so
$\gamma\ge0.75-\rho_o$ with $\rho_o=0.004$ in our organic corpus of $2{,}000$ records.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From the realised cohort to organic records}

The certificate concerns the realised cohort advantage $\Delta$, not its expectation
$\Delta_\xi$ over cohorts. The transfer costs a concentration term under the following
assumption, which holds with $\kappa=\ell=1$ under exact unlearning and is otherwise
unverifiable.

\begin{assumption}[Bounded influence]\label{ass:influence}
For fixed $\xi$, changing one coin changes at most $\kappa$ of the $m$ terms $T_i$, and
replacing the contents of one pair changes at most $\ell$ of them.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer to the generator population]\label{thm:population}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:gen} and~\ref{ass:influence}, for $\alpha'\in(0,1)$, with
probability at least $1-\alpha'$ over the coins and contents,
$|\Delta-\Delta_\xi|\le u_m(\alpha'):=\sqrt{2(\kappa^2+\ell^2)\log(2/\alpha')/m}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof applies McDiarmid's inequality \citep{mcdiarmid1989bounded} to the $2m$ independent
coordinates and is in the supplement. At $m=512$, $\alpha'=0.05$ and $\kappa=\ell=1$,
$u_m=0.17$.

\begin{corollary}[What a certificate says about organic records]\label{cor:ladder}
If a two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$ certificate is issued, then with probability at least
$1-\alpha-\alpha'$, $|\Delta^o_\xi|<\eps+u_m(\alpha')+2\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$ for every
$\mathcal H\ni\phi_\xi$. Under one-sided leakage, $\Delta^o_\xi<\eps+u_m(\alpha')+
\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$ for every $\mathcal H\ni h_\xi$, and
$\Delta^o_\xi\le\beta(\eps+u_m(\alpha'))$ with $\beta(x)=1$ for $x\ge1$.
\end{corollary}

The three terms have separate causes: the tolerance the auditor chose, the size of the
cohort, and how far the canaries are from the data they stand in for. Only the last depends
on the canary design. The auditor cannot certify it, since every detector bounds $\gamma$
from below and none from above; Section~\ref{sec:exp-detect} reports the strongest lower
bounds we obtained.
