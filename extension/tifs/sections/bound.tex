\section{What a canary-aware provider can hide}\label{sec:bound}

\subsection{Leakage profiles}

Fix a pipeline run $\xi$. We consider audits whose statistic is an average
$\Delta=\frac1m\sum_iT_i$ of per-pair terms $T_i\in[-1,1]$; for VOUCH $T_i=2Z_i-1$. Statistics
that are not averages of per-pair terms, such as an AUC over all members and non-members, the
exposure rank of a secret or the wealth of an e-process, are not covered directly. For pair
contents $c$ define the \emph{leakage profile}
\[
h_\xi(c)\;=\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,T_i\;\big|\;C_i=c,\ \xi\,\bigr]\in[-1,1],
\]
the expected term of a pair with contents $c$, the expectation being over the coins and the
other pairs' contents. The \emph{canary advantage} of the run is
$\Delta_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P}[h_\xi(c)]$, the expected statistic over fresh cohorts.

Let $P_o$ be a distribution of \emph{organic} pairs: two records of the kind users delete,
with a hypothetical fair coin choosing which was trained. The \emph{organic advantage}
$\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P_o}[h_\xi(c)]$ is the advantage the same run would show on
organic pairs planted in the same way. It is membership leakage under randomised inclusion,
evaluated on records of the kind users delete; it is a counterfactual quantity of the
pipeline, not the leakage of the particular records that users deleted, which were not
included by a coin and may be repeated or correlated.

Three conditions make $h_\xi$ well defined as a function of content alone, and we assume them
throughout. (a) The provider's treatment rule is fixed before the cohort is drawn and acts on
each record separately; features of the corpus such as the number of verbatim copies of a
record are treated as part of that record's content. A rule fitted on the realised corpus is
covered to the extent that its decisions do not depend on any single pair. (b) A planted
organic pair would be treated like any organic record, which is the no-provenance condition
of Section~\ref{sec:threat}. (c) $\xi$ is independent of the contents
(Assumption~\ref{ass:gen}).

\begin{definition}[Distinguishing advantage]\label{def:gamma}
For a class $\mathcal H$ of functions to $[0,1]$ and distributions $Q,Q_o$ on their domain,
$\gamma_{\mathcal H}(Q,Q_o)=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal H}|\mathbb{E}_Q\phi-\mathbb{E}_{Q_o}\phi|$.
For the class of all measurable functions it is the total variation distance $\TV(Q,Q_o)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The bound}

\begin{theorem}[Special-casing requires detection]\label{thm:detect}
Let $\phi_\xi=(1+h_\xi)/2\in[0,1]$. For every class $\mathcal H$ that contains $\phi_\xi$,
\[
|\Delta^o_\xi-\Delta_\xi|\;=\;2\,|\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi|
\;\le\;2\,\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By linearity, $\Delta_\xi-\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_Ph_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}h_\xi
=2(\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi)$.
\end{proof}

The proof is one line; the content is in the reading. If a run opens a gap $g$ between the
canary and the organic advantage, then $\phi_\xi$ tells canary pairs from organic pairs with
advantage $g/2$. Whatever rule the provider uses to decide what looks like a canary, the gap
it opens is at most twice that rule's advantage, and a provider that cannot recognise
canaries from their contents cannot open a gap at all. VOUCH shows that recognising the
cohort does not threaten the null, since knowing which records are canaries reveals nothing
about which twin the coin admitted \citep{dang2026vouch}. That argument concerns validity on
the cohort and is unaffected. Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} concerns whether the cohort speaks for
organic records, and there recognition is exactly what matters.

The same identity read in the other direction limits the auditor.

\begin{corollary}[Detectability--power trade-off]\label{cor:tradeoff}
For every pipeline run and every $\mathcal H\ni\phi_\xi$,
$|\Delta_\xi|\le|\Delta^o_\xi|+2\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$. In particular, if $P=P_o$, then
$\Delta_\xi=\Delta^o_\xi$ for every pipeline.
\end{corollary}

An audit is powerful when the canary advantage of a non-unlearning pipeline is large. The
corollary says that it can exceed the organic advantage of the same pipeline, in the
counterfactual sense above, only by twice the canaries' detectability. Designs that make
canaries easy to memorise, by repetition, high-entropy secrets or unusual attributes, buy
power with detectability, and canaries distributed as planted organic records are exactly as
sensitive as planted organic records.

\subsection{A sharp form for record-level providers}

The provider of Section~\ref{sec:threat} sees only the trained twin of each pair, so its
treatment of a pair is a function of one record. Call leakage \emph{record-determined and
one-sided} if there is $g_\xi:\mathcal R\to[0,1]$ on records such that
$h_\xi(c)=\frac12\bigl(g_\xi(c^0)+g_\xi(c^1)\bigr)$, that is, the expected term of a pair is
the average over the coin of a leakage $g_\xi(r)$ incurred when record $r$ is the one
trained. This holds for an idealised selective provider that either unlearns a record exactly
($g=0$) or skips it and leaves it favoured ($g=1$), and its randomised versions. Overshooting
pipelines such as gradient ascent violate one-sidedness, and Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} covers
them. By label symmetry $\Delta_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{P_1}g_\xi$ and
$\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{P_{o,1}}g_\xi$, where $P_1$ and $P_{o,1}$ are the distributions of
a single canary record and a single organic record. Let $p_1,p_{o,1}$ be their densities with
respect to $P_1+P_{o,1}$ and define the \emph{Neyman--Pearson power}
\[
\beta(\eps)=\sup_{\varphi}\bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{P_{o,1}}\varphi\;:\;\mathbb{E}_{P_1}\varphi\le\eps\bigr\},
\]
the supremum running over measurable $\varphi:\mathcal R\to[0,1]$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp evasion bound]\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\eps\in(0,1]$, $B^\ast=\{p_{o,1}>p_1\}$, $a=P_1(B^\ast)$ and $\TV=\TV(P_1,P_{o,1})$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every run with record-determined one-sided leakage and $\Delta_\xi\le\eps$ has
$\Delta^o_\xi\le\beta(\eps)\le\min\{1,\eps+\TV\}$, and $\Delta^o_\xi\le\Delta_\xi+
\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P_1,P_{o,1})$ for every $\mathcal H\ni g_\xi$.
\item[(ii)] $\beta(\eps)$ is attained by $g=\varphi^\ast$, a possibly randomised
likelihood-ratio test of $P_{o,1}$ against $P_1$, which is the leakage of a provider that
skips unlearning on record $r$ with probability $\varphi^\ast(r)$, if skipped records stay
favoured. No argument that uses only the canary advantage and the generator can exclude
$\Delta^o_\xi=\beta(\eps)$ for such a provider.
\item[(iii)] $\beta(\eps)\ge\eps(1+\TV/a)$ for $\eps\le a$ and $\beta(\eps)\ge a+\TV$ for
$\eps\ge a$. In particular $\beta(\eps)=\eps$ if $P_1=P_{o,1}$, and $\beta(\eps)=1$ for every
$\eps>0$ if $P_1$ and $P_{o,1}$ are mutually singular.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\varphi=g_\xi$ is feasible, since $\mathbb{E}_{P_1}\varphi=\Delta_\xi\le\eps$, and
$\mathbb{E}_{P_{o,1}}\varphi=\Delta^o_\xi$. For $\varphi\in[0,1]$,
$\mathbb{E}_{P_{o,1}}\varphi\le\mathbb{E}_{P_1}\varphi+\TV$; the class statement is
Definition~\ref{def:gamma}. (ii) The Neyman--Pearson lemma \citep{neyman1933efficient} gives a
likelihood-ratio test, randomised on the boundary, that attains the supremum; a provider
realises it with an independent coin per record. (iii) $\mathbf 1_{B^\ast}$ has
$\mathbb{E}_{P_1}=a$ and $\mathbb{E}_{P_{o,1}}=a+\TV$; for $\eps\le a$ use $(\eps/a)\mathbf
1_{B^\ast}$. If $P_1=P_{o,1}$, (i) gives $\beta\le\eps$ and $\varphi\equiv\eps$ attains it. If
$P_1\perp P_{o,1}$, then $a=0$ and $P_{o,1}(B^\ast)=1$.
\end{proof}

The record-level distance is at most the pair-level one, so the record form is the stronger
statement for this provider. Sharpness is relative to the idealised profile in which a
skipped record stays favoured; a real model memorises a skipped record only partly, so a real
provider hides less organic leakage per skipped record, but it can also skip more records for
the same canary advantage.

\paragraph{Every detector is an attack} Part (ii) turns any canary detector into a provider
strategy. A provider that unlearns the records its detector flags and skips the rest has a
canary advantage of at most the detector's miss rate on canaries, with equality in the
idealised profile, and skips the fraction of organic records that the detector passes. Its
gain over a provider that skips records at random, which skips a fraction equal to the miss
rate, estimates $\beta(\eps)-\eps$ from below. Conversely every provider strategy is a
detector. A certificate needs the realised advantage strictly below $\eps$ with a margin, so
a miss rate of $\eps$ is the edge of what the audit tolerates, not a point it certainly
passes.

\begin{remark}[Repetition is content]\label{rem:dup}
The provider sees the corpus, so a canary repeated verbatim is flagged by the duplicate
detector $\phi(r)=\mathbf 1[r\text{ occurs at least twice}]$, whose advantage is
$P_1(\text{repeated})-\rho_o$ with $\rho_o$ the share of duplicated organic records. Designs
that repeat canaries to strengthen memorisation pay before any content is examined. The
semantic design of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} repeats about three quarters of its twins,
so $\gamma\gtrsim0.75-\rho_o$ with $\rho_o=0.004$ in our organic corpus of $2{,}000$ records.
VOUCH's random-string design is already separated by its content.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From the realised cohort to organic records}

The certificate concerns the realised cohort advantage $\Delta$, not its expectation
$\Delta_\xi$ over cohorts. The transfer costs a concentration term under the following
assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Bounded influence]\label{ass:influence}
For fixed $\xi$, changing one coin changes at most $\kappa$ of the $m$ terms $T_i$, and
replacing the contents of one pair changes at most $\ell$ of them.
\end{assumption}

Under exact unlearning $\kappa=\ell=1$. Under partial or selective unlearning, the regime of
interest here, one record's training can perturb every pair whose score difference is close
to zero, the assumption cannot be verified, and with $\ell$ of order $m$ the term below is
vacuous.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer to the generator population]\label{thm:population}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:gen} and~\ref{ass:influence}, for $\alpha'\in(0,1)$, with
probability at least $1-\alpha'$ over the coins and contents,
$|\Delta-\Delta_\xi|\le u_m(\alpha'):=\sqrt{2(\kappa^2+\ell^2)\log(2/\alpha')/m}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof applies McDiarmid's inequality \citep{mcdiarmid1989bounded} and is in the
supplement. At $m=384$, $\alpha'=0.05$ and $\kappa=\ell=1$, $u_m=0.20$, as large as the
tolerance $\eps=0.2$ used in our experiments; the term halves only at four times the cohort.

\begin{corollary}[What a certificate says about organic records]\label{cor:ladder}
Under the conditions above, the probability that a two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$ certificate is
issued and $|\Delta^o_\xi|\ge\eps+u_m(\alpha')+2\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$ for some
$\mathcal H\ni\phi_\xi$ is at most $\alpha+\alpha'$. Under record-determined one-sided leakage
the same holds with $\Delta^o_\xi\ge\eps+u_m(\alpha')+\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P_1,P_{o,1})$ for
some $\mathcal H\ni g_\xi$, and with $\Delta^o_\xi>\beta(\eps+u_m(\alpha'))$, where
$\beta(x)=1$ for $x\ge1$.
\end{corollary}

This is a joint probability: when certificates are rarely issued, the probability of the
event given issuance can be larger. The three terms have separate causes, the tolerance, the
cohort size and the distance between canaries and the data they stand in for. Only the last
depends on the canary design. The auditor cannot certify it, since a detector bounds $\gamma$
only from below; Section~\ref{sec:exp-detect} reports estimates for the detectors we built.
