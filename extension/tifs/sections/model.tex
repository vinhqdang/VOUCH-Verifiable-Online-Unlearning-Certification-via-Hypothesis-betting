\section{Audit and threat model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Parties and pipeline}

A \emph{provider} fine-tunes a model on a corpus, receives deletion requests, applies an
unlearning routine to a forget set and deploys the resulting model $M$. An \emph{auditor}
wants evidence that the routine removed the influence of the forget set without trusting
the provider's report. Before training, the auditor generates a cohort of $m$ canary pairs
with contents $C_i=(c_i^0,c_i^1)$, $i=1,\dots,m$, and draws independent fair coins
$b_i\sim\Bern(\frac12)$. The \emph{in-twin} $c_i^{b_i}$ is inserted into the training corpus
and later into the forget set; the \emph{ghost twin} $c_i^{1-b_i}$ is never trained on. The
auditor commits to the manifest of contents and coins (VOUCH uses a Merkle commitment) and
opens a coin only when its pair is audited. All other randomness of training and
unlearning is collected in $\xi$.

\begin{assumption}[Generator]\label{ass:gen}
The contents $C_1,\dots,C_m$ are independent draws from a distribution $P$ on ordered pairs
that is label-symmetric: $(c^0,c^1)\sim P$ implies $(c^1,c^0)\sim P$. The coins are
independent of the contents and of $\xi$, and $\xi$ is independent of the contents.
\end{assumption}

Both properties describe the auditor's own sampler, not the model or the data, and are
under the auditor's control. Label symmetry makes the two twins of a pair identically
distributed, so every canary record is a draw from one record distribution.

\subsection{The audit}

A \emph{score} $s(M,c)$ measures how much the model favours a record, for example the mean
token log-probability of a secret value after its prompt. A class $\F$ of scores is declared
before any query. For each pair and score the auditor computes
$D_i^{(s)}=s(M,c_i^{\mathrm{in}})-s(M,c_i^{\mathrm{ghost}})$ and its sign
$Z_i^{(s)}=\mathbf 1[D_i^{(s)}>0]$, with ties split by a committed coin. The \emph{cohort
advantage} of a score is
\[
\Delta \;=\; \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\bigl(2Z_i-1\bigr)\in[-1,1].
\]
It is zero in expectation under exact unlearning and positive when the in-twins are still
favoured; negative values, in-twins scored conspicuously low, are leakage as well.

\paragraph{The exact null.} If $M$ is conditionally independent of the coins given the
contents, as under exact unlearning or retraining from scratch, the sign of the pair opened
at each step of a reveal order chosen independently of the signs is a fair coin given
everything opened before \cite[Thm.~1]{dang2026vouch}. No independence between pairs is
needed.

\paragraph{The certificate.} VOUCH reveals the pairs in a uniformly random order and runs,
for each score and direction, a betting wealth process against the boundary
$p_0(t)=(mp_0-S_{t-1})/(m-t+1)$ with $p_0=\frac12+\frac\eps2$, where $S_{t-1}$ counts the
ones already revealed. If the cohort advantage were at least $\eps$, each wealth process
would be a nonnegative supermartingale, so by Ville's inequality the probability that any
of them ever reaches $1/\alpha$ is at most $\alpha$. A two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$
certificate (ISSUED) states $|\Delta|<\eps$ for every declared score and is false with
probability at most $\alpha$ under arbitrary dependence between pairs
\cite[Thm.~2]{dang2026vouch}. A separate process tests the exact null and raises an alarm
(REVOKED) when the model still depends on the coins; a run that does neither is
UNDETERMINED. We use VOUCH as the running example, but the results of
Section~\ref{sec:bound} hold for any audit statistic that averages a bounded per-pair
leakage over randomly included canaries, for example a paired membership-inference test
whose per-pair statistic is a bounded function of the twins' score difference.

\subsection{Threat model}\label{sec:threat}

The provider runs the committed training and unlearning pipeline on the committed data,
but the pipeline may treat records differently according to their content. Formally, the
provider chooses, before seeing any coin, a measurable rule that maps each record to the
treatment it receives (for example, how many unlearning steps act on it, or whether it
is unlearned at all). We call such a provider \emph{canary-aware}. Its goal is to pass the
audit, that is, to keep the canary advantage below the tolerance $\eps$, while leaving as
much organic data in the forget set as possible un-unlearned.

\emph{Capabilities.} The provider sees every record of the training corpus and of the forget
set, including canaries, and may hold labelled examples of the auditor's canary design, for
instance because the generator is public. It may count repetitions in the corpus. It sees
neither the coins nor which twin of a pair was trained, because only the in-twin enters
its data.

\emph{Out of scope.} A provider that recognises canaries by the channel through which they
arrive (provenance) defeats any content-based guarantee; the auditor must inject canaries
through the ordinary data path, for example as ordinary user accounts. A provider that runs
a different pipeline than the committed one, or serves a different model to the auditor,
is addressed by cryptographic proofs of unlearning \citep{eisenhofer2025verifiable} and is
not considered.

\emph{A second failure mode.} Independently of strategy, a deletion request may name one
record of a fact that the model also learned from another record, and gradient-based
unlearning can suppress a trained string while the fact stays reachable through paraphrase
\citep{li2026beliefs}. An audit that scores only the trained surface form cannot see such
residue. We treat this as a property of the declared score class and return to it in
Sections~\ref{sec:design} and~\ref{sec:exp-squeeze}.
