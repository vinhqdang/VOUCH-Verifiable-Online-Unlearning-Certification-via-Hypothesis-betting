\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Recommendations for auditors} Four follow from the results. First, a
planted-canary certificate speaks for organic data only up to the canaries' detectability,
and detectability should be measured and reported with every audit; for repeated or
high-entropy canaries it is close to one and the certificate is a statement about the canaries
alone. Second, canaries must enter through the ordinary data path, since provenance defeats
any content-based guarantee. Third, a membership-inference audit should compare members with
non-members drawn from one distribution by construction, as ghost twins are, and should pair
them. Fourth, the declared class should include paraphrase scores next to the literal score,
which costs nothing in certificate error and catches residue that an incomplete forget set
leaves behind.

\paragraph{The trade-off cannot be designed away} Corollary~\ref{cor:tradeoff} is an identity,
not an artefact of our designs: an audit can see more than the counterfactual organic advantage
only through features that also make its canaries recognisable. A certificate from
indistinguishable canaries speaks for organic records planted in the same way, whose leakage at
single exposure may be small, and then cannot distinguish unlearning from never having learned.
It does not speak for the particular records users deleted, which may have been seen many
times. Larger training
budgets, canaries spread over several slots, or repetition through paraphrased rather than
verbatim copies are ways to buy back power, each at a detectability cost that would have to
be measured.

\paragraph{Detectability is bounded only from below} Our detectors are two cheap families; a
provider with more compute could train stronger ones, and the organic statement is conditional
on $\gamma$. What the theorem establishes is the direction of the burden: a provider that
passes on canaries while leaving organic data untouched has, in effect, built a canary
detector, and the gap it obtains is bounded by that detector's advantage. Generating matched
canaries for real corpora, whose distribution the auditor sees only through samples, is the
main open problem.

\paragraph{Scope of the evaluation} The selective provider is simulated from detector decisions
on records, which determines its canary and organic advantages in the idealised profile of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}; we did not train and selectively unlearn a model end to end, so no
audit verdict for a selective provider is reported. The detectors are trained on canaries from
the same generators and on organic records from the same corpus, and the canaries of other
audits were not tested.
All facts are fictitious and from one domain, the models have at most one billion parameters
and are fine-tuned with LoRA for $600$ steps, and the same unlearning step counts are used for
both sizes. Per-example calibrated attacks with shadow models \citep{carlini2022lira} were
compared with VOUCH in the original paper and are not repeated.

\paragraph{Scope of the theory} Theorem~\ref{thm:population} conditions on the training
randomness and assumes bounded influence, which holds under exact unlearning but cannot be
verified under partial unlearning. The organic advantage is counterfactual. The sharp form
needs one-sided leakage, which gradient ascent violates; Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} with its
factor two covers that case. Proposition~\ref{S-prop:anytime} assumes i.i.d.\ signs and an
oracle stake.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A planted-canary audit certifies the canaries. Whether it certifies anything about the data
users delete depends on whether the provider can tell the two apart, and we proved that the
leakage a canary-aware provider can hide is at most twice its distinguishing advantage, exactly
the Neyman--Pearson power when leakage is one-sided. The two canary designs we tested are
recognised perfectly, and in simulation a provider guided by a simple classifier skips at least
$99\%$ of organic records while holding its canary miss rate at the tolerance. Organic-matched
canaries, which keep the exact null, are not separated by our detectors beyond noise, but they
can be no more sensitive than planted organic records, and at single exposure the audit had no
power. An auditor must
choose a point on this trade-off and report it; membership-inference audits against unmatched
holdouts measure distribution shift rather than membership and should not be used.
