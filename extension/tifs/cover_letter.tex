\begin{letter}{Editor-in-Chief\\IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security}
\opening{Dear Editor-in-Chief,}

I submit the manuscript ``Canary-Aware Providers and the Limits of Unlearning Audits: A Sharp
Bound and the Detectability--Power Trade-off'' for consideration as a regular paper.

\textbf{Problem.} Audits of machine unlearning plant canary records in a model's training
data, include them in a deletion request, and test whether the deployed model still favours
them. Such an audit certifies the canaries. It certifies the data users delete only if the
provider cannot treat the two differently, an assumption that current audits make and do not
test. The manuscript studies a provider that recognises canaries by their content and
unlearns them while retaining other data.

\textbf{Contributions and significance.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A bound, valid for any audit that compares randomly included canaries with excluded
twins, under which the leakage a canary-aware provider can hide is at most twice the
advantage of the best canary detector, together with a sharp form: for one-sided leakage
the largest hideable leakage equals the Neyman--Pearson power of telling canaries from
organic records. Every detector is therefore an attack, and every attack is a detector.
\item A measurement on current designs: the canaries of a recent anytime-valid audit are
recognised perfectly by a character $n$-gram classifier, and a provider guided by it holds
the canary advantage at the audit's tolerance while leaving all organic records
un-unlearned.
\item A canary design drawn from the organic record generator, with a proof that it keeps the
audit's exact null, which reduces the provider's gain to near chance; and a
detectability--power trade-off that follows from the bound and is confirmed on Pythia
models: indistinguishable canaries are memorised no more than organic records, so the audit
becomes valid but powerless.
\item Two practical failure modes: a membership-inference audit against an unmatched holdout
accuses an honest, retrained provider in every run, and a literal-only audit misses residue
that an incomplete forget set leaves behind.
\end{itemize}
The results give auditors and regulators a concrete rule: a canary-based unlearning
certificate speaks for user data only up to the canaries' measured detectability, which
should be reported with every audit.

\textbf{Closest prior work and what is new.} The closest work is my own paper on VOUCH,
``VOUCH: Anytime-Valid Certificates for Machine Unlearning via Paired-Canary
Hypothesis-Betting'', accepted by \emph{Machine Learning} (Springer) and in press. VOUCH
introduced the paired-canary certificate that the manuscript uses as its running example,
and it assumes that the provider does not special-case canaries. The present manuscript drops
that assumption; the evasion bound and its sharp form, the selective-provider evaluation, the
semantic and organic-matched canary designs, the trade-off and the membership-inference
comparison are all new and do not appear in the VOUCH paper. Because the VOUCH paper is not
yet publicly available, its accepted version is uploaded with this submission for the
reviewers' reference. Related work on dishonest providers (Zhang et al., 2024), canary-based
privacy auditing (Steinke et al., 2023), copyright traps (Meeus et al., 2024) and
distribution shift in membership-inference benchmarks (Duan et al., 2024; Das et al., 2024)
is discussed in Section~VI; none of these bounds what recognising canaries buys a provider.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} All code, per-seed results and the scripts that produce every
table and figure are public at
\url{https://github.com/vinhqdang/VOUCH-Verifiable-Online-Unlearning-Certification-via-Hypothesis-betting}
(directory \texttt{extension/}); all records are fictitious and generated from committed
seeds.
