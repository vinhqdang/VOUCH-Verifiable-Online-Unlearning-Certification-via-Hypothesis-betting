\section{The price of anytime validity}\label{sec:anytime}

A fixed-sample test is run once, at a sample size fixed in advance, and is invalid if the
verifier looks earlier. VOUCH's tests can be monitored continuously and stopped at any
time. What does that cost in samples? The question has a clean answer in the model that
underlies the signs: i.i.d.\ $Z_t\sim\Bern(\frac{1+\Delta}{2})$ for an advantage
$\Delta\in(0,1)$, tested against the exact null $\Delta=0$. Write
$\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)=\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\frac{1+\Delta}2\,\|\,\frac12\bigr)=\frac{1+\Delta}2
\log(1+\Delta)+\frac{1-\Delta}2\log(1-\Delta)$, which is $\Delta^2/2+O(\Delta^4)$.

\begin{proposition}[Expected stopping time]\label{prop:anytime}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\Delta\in(0,1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Lower bound.} Let $\tau$ be a stopping time with $\Pr_0[\tau<\infty]\le
\alpha$ (a level-$\alpha$ sequential test that rejects at $\tau$) and $\Pr_\Delta[\tau<
\infty]=1$, $\mathbb{E}_\Delta\tau<\infty$. Then
$\mathbb{E}_\Delta[\tau]\ge\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Upper bound for the oracle bettor.} The wealth
$W_t=\prod_{u\le t}\bigl(1+\Delta(2Z_u-1)\bigr)$ is a martingale under the null with growth
rate $\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)$ under $\Delta$, and $\tau=\inf\{t:W_t\ge1/\alpha\}$ is a
level-$\alpha$ test with
\[
\mathbb{E}_\Delta[\tau]\;\le\;\frac{\log(1/\alpha)+\log(1+\Delta)}{\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)} .
\]
\item[(iii)] \emph{Grid mixture.} The uniform mixture of the constant-stake bettors with
stakes $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_K$ has, for every $k$ with $g_\Delta(\lambda_k)>0$,
$\mathbb{E}_\Delta[\tau]\le\bigl(\log(K/\alpha)+\log(1+\lambda_k)\bigr)/g_\Delta(\lambda_k)$,
where $g_\Delta(\lambda)=\frac{1+\Delta}2\log(1+\lambda)+\frac{1-\Delta}2\log(1-\lambda)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $\Lambda_t=\sum_{u\le t}\log\frac{d\Bern((1+\Delta)/2)}{d\Bern(1/2)}(Z_u)$. Wald's
identity \citep{wald1945sequential} gives $\mathbb{E}_\Delta\Lambda_\tau=\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)\,\mathbb{E}_\Delta\tau$.
Changing measure on $\{\tau=n\}$, where $d\Pr_0/d\Pr_\Delta=e^{-\Lambda_n}$,
$\alpha\ge\Pr_0[\tau<\infty]=\mathbb{E}_\Delta\bigl[e^{-\Lambda_\tau}\bigr]\ge
e^{-\mathbb{E}_\Delta\Lambda_\tau}$ by Jensen, so $\mathbb{E}_\Delta\Lambda_\tau\ge
\log(1/\alpha)$.
(ii) $W_t$ is a nonnegative martingale under the null with $W_0=1$, so Ville's inequality
gives level $\alpha$. Under $\Delta$, $\mathbb{E}\log(1+\Delta(2Z-1))=\frac{1+\Delta}2
\log(1+\Delta)+\frac{1-\Delta}2\log(1-\Delta)=\mathrm{KL}(\Delta)=:g$, and
$\log W_t-gt$ is a martingale with bounded increments, so Wald's identity gives
$\mathbb{E}\log W_\tau=g\,\mathbb{E}\tau$ once $\mathbb{E}\tau<\infty$, which holds since the
drift is positive. Since $W_{\tau-1}<1/\alpha$ and each factor is at most $1+\Delta$,
$\log W_\tau<\log(1/\alpha)+\log(1+\Delta)$.
(iii) The mixture wealth is at least $W^{\lambda_k}_t/K$, so it crosses $1/\alpha$ no later
than $W^{\lambda_k}$ crosses $K/\alpha$; apply (ii) with $\alpha\to\alpha/K$ and the stake
$\lambda_k$, whose growth rate is $g_\Delta(\lambda_k)$.
\end{proof}

The oracle bettor knows $\Delta$; a real bettor does not, and part (iii) and the
simulations below price that ignorance. Part (i) shows that no sequential test, however
clever, beats $\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}$, and part (ii) that the oracle comes within
$\log(1+\Delta)/\mathrm{KL}$ of it.

\paragraph{Against a fixed-sample test.} A two-sided sequential test runs two arms at level
$\alpha/2$, so its oracle expected time is $2\log(2/\alpha)/\Delta^2\,(1+o(1))$ as
$\Delta\to0$. The two-sided fixed-sample sign test at level $\alpha$ needs
$n\approx(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})^2/\Delta^2$ pairs for power $1-\beta$. At
$\alpha=0.05$ the constants are $2\log(40)=7.4$ for the sequential oracle and $3.8$, $7.9$,
$10.5$ for power $0.5$, $0.8$, $0.9$: the oracle's expected time equals the sample size of
a fixed-sample test with about $78\%$ power, and it can stop earlier when $\Delta$ is large
and is valid under any stopping rule. Table~\ref{tab:anytime} confirms the
constants by simulation and adds the mixture over the ten stakes
$0.05,0.10,\dots,0.50$ that the cohort analysis of Section~\ref{sec:time} uses, which costs
$1.1$ to $1.4$ times the oracle, more at small $\Delta$.

This explains a result of Section~\ref{sec:exp-baselines}. A paired fixed-sample sign test
on the same pairs and the same score is about as powerful as VOUCH: the price of anytime
validity is a constant factor of order one, not an order of magnitude. What the sequential
test adds is that it may be monitored continuously and stopped at will without inflating
the error, which the fixed-sample test may not. The cohort certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:time} is faster again, because its edge grows as the cohort is exhausted.
