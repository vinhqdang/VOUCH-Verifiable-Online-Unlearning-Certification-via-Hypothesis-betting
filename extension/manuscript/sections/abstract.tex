VOUCH certifies machine unlearning with paired ghost canaries: a fair coin decides which
of two exchangeable records is trained and later unlearned, which makes the sign of their
score difference an exact fair coin under exact unlearning, and betting e-processes turn
these signs into an anytime-valid certificate on the committed cohort. The certificate
was limited to random-string canaries and to a provider trusted not to treat canaries
differently from other data. We extend it in three directions. First, semantic twins, two
incompatible natural-language facts about one fictitious entity, keep the exact null for
every score that compares the two facts under the same prompt, including belief scores
over paraphrased questions never seen in training. Second, under a bounded-influence
assumption the cohort certificate transfers to the canary generator's population with a
McDiarmid term, and from canaries to organic records with a term equal to twice the
provider's ability to tell them apart from their contents, which prices content-based
special-casing, and for one-sided leakage the bound is exact and equals a Neyman--Pearson
power. VOUCH's random-string canaries and naive semantic twins are told apart with
advantage one; twins drawn from the organic record generator are not, but on Pythia-410M at our
training budget a record seen once leaves no detectable trace, so indistinguishability
costs the audit its power. Third, on Pythia-410M and Pythia-1B, when unlearning targets only the literal
statement, the advantage on paraphrased questions is retained slightly longer than the
literal one ($+0.07$ to $+0.12$ in like-for-like retention), consistently in direction
but not significantly at eight seeds. When the forget set is incomplete, an audit that
adds calibrated paraphrase scores revokes up to $38\%$ of runs that a literal-only audit
certifies, driven by a minority of seeds; when it is complete, the two audits agree. On the same runs, a
membership-inference test against an organic holdout raises a false alarm on every
retrained model, and against matched ghost twins it is valid but far less powerful than a paired test on
the same records, which is where VOUCH's advantage comes from. In the sign model we show that
no sequential test needs fewer than $\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}$ samples and that an oracle
bettor comes within a logarithmic term of that, comparable to a fixed-sample sign test at
$78\%$ power, and we bound the cohort's certification time through the growth of the wealth
process.
