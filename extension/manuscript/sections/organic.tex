\section{From the cohort to organic records}\label{sec:organic}

The VOUCH certificate concerns the realised advantage $\Delta$ on one committed cohort.
This section climbs a ladder of three targets: the cohort, the generator population,
and organic records, the data users actually delete. Each step costs one term in the
tolerance, and each term has its own cause.

\subsection{From the cohort to the generator population}

VOUCH Proposition~3 averages over the coins with the contents fixed. The verifier also
controls the contents, through Assumption~\ref{ass:gen}, so the same argument reaches
them.

\begin{assumption}[Bounded influence]\label{ass:influence}
For fixed $\xi$, changing a single coin $b_i$ changes at most $\kappa$ of the $m$
signs, and replacing the contents $C_i$ of a single pair changes at most $\ell$ of
them.
\end{assumption}

Under exact unlearning $\kappa=\ell=1$: the model carries no trace of the forget set,
so a pair's coin or contents can affect only that pair's own sign. Under partial
unlearning $\kappa$ and $\ell$ measure how far one canary's training reaches into the
others' scores. The assumption is a worst-case bound that the verifier cannot check:
with stochastic training, changing one training record perturbs the weights and can
flip the sign of every pair whose score difference is close to zero. It is plausible
for canaries about distinct fictitious entities, whose score differences are dominated
by their own training, but we state Theorem~\ref{thm:population} as a conditional
result and use it only to explain how each term of the ladder arises.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer to the generator population]\label{thm:population}
Let $\Delta_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{b,C}[\Delta\mid\xi]$ be the expected advantage of this
pipeline run over fresh cohorts from $P$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:gen}
and~\ref{ass:influence}, for any $\alpha'\in(0,1)$, with probability at least
$1-\alpha'$ over $(b,C)$,
\[
|\Delta-\Delta_\xi| \;\le\; u_m(\alpha') \;:=\;
\sqrt{\frac{2(\kappa^2+\ell^2)\log(2/\alpha')}{m}} .
\]
Consequently a two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$ cohort certificate implies
$|\Delta_\xi|<\eps+u_m(\alpha')$ with probability at least $1-\alpha-\alpha'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given $\xi$, $\Delta$ is a function of the $2m$ independent coordinates
$(b_1,\dots,b_m,C_1,\dots,C_m)$. By Assumption~\ref{ass:influence}, changing one coin
changes $\Delta$ by at most $2\kappa/m$ and changing one pair's contents by at most
$2\ell/m$. McDiarmid's inequality \citep{mcdiarmid1989bounded} gives
$\Pr[|\Delta-\Delta_\xi|\ge u]\le 2\exp(-2u^2/\sum_k c_k^2)$ with
$\sum_k c_k^2=4(\kappa^2+\ell^2)/m$, that is
$2\exp\bigl(-u^2m/(2(\kappa^2+\ell^2))\bigr)$; equating it to $\alpha'$ gives $u_m$. The
consequence follows from a union bound with the certificate's error probability.
\end{proof}

$\Delta_\xi$ averages over cohorts but conditions on the training randomness $\xi$: it
is the advantage of \emph{this} deployed pipeline run on the generator population, the
relevant target for an auditor of a deployed model. Averaging over $\xi$ would need
several independent training runs and is not attempted. At $m=512$, $\alpha'=0.05$ and
$\kappa=\ell=1$ the inflation is $u_m=0.17$, against $0.12$ for the coin-only transfer.

\subsection{From canaries to organic records}

Let $P_o$ be a distribution of \emph{organic} record pairs: two records of the kind a
user deletes, with a hypothetical fair coin choosing which one was trained on. For pair
contents $c$, let
\[
h_\xi(c) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[\,2Z_i-1 \;\big|\; C_i=c,\ \xi\,\bigr]
\]
be the expected sign contribution of a pair with contents $c$ placed in the cohort,
the expectation being over the coins and the other pairs' contents. Then
$\Delta_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P}[h_\xi(c)]$, and we define the \emph{organic advantage}
$\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_{c\sim P_o}[h_\xi(c)]$: the advantage the same pipeline run
would show on organic pairs planted in the same way. This is the standard notion of
membership leakage under randomised inclusion, evaluated on the data users delete;
because organic records were not in fact included by a coin, it is a counterfactual
quantity of the pipeline, not an observed rate.

\begin{definition}[Distinguishing advantage]\label{def:gamma}
For a class $\mathcal H$ of functions from pair contents to $[0,1]$,
$\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal H}\bigl|\mathbb{E}_P\phi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi\bigr|$.
When $\mathcal H$ contains all measurable functions, $\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ is the total
variation distance between $P$ and $P_o$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Special-casing requires detection]\label{thm:detect}
Let $\phi_\xi=(1+h_\xi)/2$, which takes values in $[0,1]$. Then
\[
|\Delta^o_\xi-\Delta_\xi| \;=\; 2\,\bigl|\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi\bigr|
\;\le\; 2\,\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)
\]
for every class $\mathcal H$ that contains $\phi_\xi$. Equivalently, if the pipeline
treats canaries and organic records differently enough to open a gap $g$ between their
advantages, then $\phi_\xi$ is a test that tells canary pairs from organic pairs with
advantage at least $g/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By linearity, $\Delta_\xi-\Delta^o_\xi=\mathbb{E}_Ph_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}h_\xi
=2(\mathbb{E}_P\phi_\xi-\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\phi_\xi)$, and the right-hand side is at most
$2\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ in absolute value whenever $\phi_\xi\in\mathcal H$.
\end{proof}

The proof is one line; the content is in the reading. A gap between the canary and the
organic advantage of one pipeline run can exist only if the run treats the two kinds of
record differently, and $\phi_\xi$ is the function that describes the difference. The
theorem therefore prices one specific deviation from VOUCH's honest-but-verifiable
threat model, \emph{content-based special-casing}: a provider that unlearns records more
thoroughly when they look like canaries. Whatever rule the provider uses to decide what
looks like a canary, the gap it opens is at most twice that rule's advantage at telling
canaries from organic records, so a provider that cannot recognise canaries from their
contents cannot open a gap at all. Two qualifications apply. The theorem is an upper
bound over all pipelines, and Section~\ref{sec:sharp} makes it sharp over one-sided
leakage profiles. And it does not cover special-casing by provenance: if canaries reach the provider through a
channel that organic data does not use, the provider can recognise them without looking
at their contents, and the verifier must inject canaries through the ordinary data path
for the theorem to say anything.

The original VOUCH paper argues that locating the cohort does not threaten the null,
because a provider that knows which records are canaries learns nothing about which
twin the coin admitted. That argument concerns the validity of the certificate on the
cohort and is unaffected here. Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} concerns a different question,
whether the certificate on the cohort says anything about organic records, and there
the ability to locate the cohort is exactly what matters. Three consequences follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If the manifest is committed but its contents are hidden until after deployment,
the provider can recognise canaries only from their contents, which is what
$\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ measures.
\item An auditor cannot certify $\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ from data. Every detector we train
gives a \emph{lower} bound; none gives an upper bound. We therefore report the strongest
detectors we could build and state the organic claim conditionally on $\gamma$.
\item Making $P$ close to $P_o$ shrinks $\gamma$ against every detector at once. In the
limit where each canary record is drawn from the organic record distribution itself,
$\gamma=0$ at the level of single records.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{corollary}[The ladder]\label{cor:ladder}
Under the assumptions of VOUCH Theorem~2 and of Theorems~\ref{thm:population}
and~\ref{thm:detect}, if a two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$ cohort certificate is issued, then
with probability at least $1-\alpha-\alpha'$,
\[
|\Delta^o_\xi| \;<\; \eps \;+\; u_m(\alpha') \;+\; 2\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o).
\]
\end{corollary}

The three terms have separate causes: the tolerance the auditor chose, the size of the
cohort, and how far the canaries are from the data they stand in for. Only the third
depends on the canary design, and Section~\ref{sec:exp-detect} measures it.

\subsection{A sharp form for one-sided leakage}\label{sec:sharp}

The factor two in Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} comes from allowing over-forgetting: $h_\xi$
ranges over $[-1,1]$. Call leakage \emph{one-sided} when $h_\xi(c)\ge0$ for every
content $c$, that is, no record is forgotten beyond exactness, so that $h_\xi$ takes values
in $[0,1]$. This is a pointwise assumption about the pipeline that an auditor cannot check.
It holds for an idealised selective provider that either leaves a record alone or unlearns
it exactly, and for pipelines that provably do not over-forget. It need not hold for
gradient-ascent objectives, which over-forget (the original paper reports certificates
blocked by the lower arm), and for those Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} and its factor two
apply. Under one-sided leakage the bound becomes an equality of optimal values. Let $p$ and $p_o$
be the densities of $P$ and $P_o$ with respect to $\mu=P+P_o$,
$\mathrm{TV}=\tfrac12\int|p-p_o|\,d\mu$, and define the \emph{Neyman--Pearson power}
\[
\beta(\eps)\;=\;\sup\Bigl\{\,\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi\;:\;\varphi:\mathcal C\to[0,1]
\text{ measurable},\ \mathbb{E}_{P}\varphi\le\eps\Bigr\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp transfer for one-sided leakage]\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\eps\in(0,1]$ and let $B^\ast=\{p_o>p\}$, $a=P(B^\ast)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every pipeline run with $h_\xi\in[0,1]$ and $\Delta_\xi\le\eps$ has
$\Delta^o_\xi\le\beta(\eps)\le\min\{1,\ \eps+\mathrm{TV}(P,P_o)\}$. If, more generally,
$h_\xi\in\mathcal H\subseteq[0,1]^{\mathcal C}$, then $\Delta^o_\xi\le\Delta_\xi+
\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$.
\item[(ii)] The value $\beta(\eps)$ is attained by a leakage profile $h=\varphi^\ast$ with
$\varphi^\ast$ a likelihood-ratio test of $P_o$ against $P$; hence no argument that uses
only the cohort advantage and the generator can exclude $\Delta^o_\xi=\beta(\eps)$.
If $P$ has no atoms on $\{p>0\}$, $\varphi^\ast$ can be taken to be an indicator of a set
of records; otherwise it is fractional. An indicator profile is the leakage of a selective
provider that skips unlearning on that set, provided the records it skips are always
favoured, $h=1$.
\item[(iii)] $\beta(\eps)\ge\eps\,(1+\mathrm{TV}/a)$ for $\eps\le a$ and
$\beta(\eps)\ge a+\mathrm{TV}$ for $\eps\ge a$. In particular $\beta(\eps)=\eps$ if
$P=P_o$, and $\beta(\eps)=1$ for every $\eps>0$ if $P$ and $P_o$ are mutually singular.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The function $\varphi=h_\xi$ is feasible for the supremum, since
$\mathbb{E}_P\varphi=\Delta_\xi\le\eps$ and $\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi=\Delta^o_\xi$. For
$\varphi\in[0,1]$, $\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi\le\mathbb{E}_{P}\varphi+\mathrm{TV}$, which gives
$\beta(\eps)\le\eps+\mathrm{TV}$, and $\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi\le1$. The statement for a
class $\mathcal H$ is the definition of $\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ applied to $\varphi=h_\xi$.
(ii) The Neyman--Pearson lemma \citep{neyman1933efficient} gives a (possibly randomised) likelihood-ratio test attaining
the supremum; if $P$ is atomless, a non-randomised test of the same size and power exists.
(iii) $\varphi=\mathbf 1_{B^\ast}$ has $\mathbb{E}_P\varphi=a$ and
$\mathbb{E}_{P_o}\varphi=a+\mathrm{TV}$, which is feasible for $\eps\ge a$; for $\eps\le a$
use $(\eps/a)\mathbf 1_{B^\ast}$, with $\mathbb{E}_{P_o}=(\eps/a)(a+\mathrm{TV})$. If
$P=P_o$ then $\mathrm{TV}=0$ and (i) gives $\beta(\eps)\le\eps$, while $\varphi\equiv\eps$
attains it. If $P\perp P_o$ then $a=0$ and $\mathbf 1_{B^\ast}$, with $P_o(B^\ast)=1$, is
feasible for every $\eps>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The ladder, one-sided leakage]\label{cor:ladder1}
Under the assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:ladder}, if the leakage is one-sided and a
two-sided $(\eps,\alpha)$ cohort certificate is issued, then with probability at least
$1-\alpha-\alpha'$, $\Delta^o_\xi<\eps+u_m(\alpha')+\gamma_{\mathcal H}(P,P_o)$ whenever
$h_\xi\in\mathcal H$, and $\Delta^o_\xi\le\beta(\eps+u_m(\alpha'))$, with the convention
$\beta(x)=1$ for $x\ge1$.
\end{corollary}

The closed forms $\eps+\mathrm{TV}$ and $\Delta+\gamma_{\mathcal H}$ are upper bounds; the
quantity that is sharp is $\beta(\eps)$, and $\eps+\mathrm{TV}$ is attained only at
$\eps=a$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} puts numbers on the two ends of the design space that the
experiments explore. For random-string canaries and for semantic twins built from their
own templates, $P$ and $P_o$ are separated by a trained detector with advantage one, so
$\beta(\eps)$ is close to $1$: a selective provider that holds labelled canaries can keep
the cohort advantage near $\eps$ while leaving essentially every organic record
un-unlearned, and the certificate says nothing about organic data. For organic-matched
twins with $P=P_o$, $\beta(\eps)=\eps$ and the certificate transfers exactly,
$\Delta^o_\xi\le\eps$ up to the generator term; with $\gamma>0$ it transfers up to
$\eps+\gamma$, and we can bound $\gamma$ only from below. Between the two, any detector
gives a lower bound on $\beta(\eps)$: a provider that unlearns the records its detector
calls canary-like and leaves the rest has cohort advantage at most the detector's miss rate
on canaries, with equality if the skipped canaries are always favoured, and leaves
un-unlearned the fraction of organic records equal to the detector's true-negative rate at
that miss rate. A cohort certificate needs the realised advantage to be strictly below
$\eps$ with a margin, so a miss rate of $\eps$ is the boundary of what the audit tolerates,
not a point it certainly passes.
Section~\ref{sec:exp-detect} measures this quantity for our detectors.

\begin{remark}[Repetition is part of the content]
The provider sees the training corpus, so a canary that is repeated verbatim is recognised
by the duplicate detector $\phi(c)=\mathbf 1[\text{$c$ occurs at least twice}]$, whose
advantage is $P(\text{canary repeated})-\rho_o$, where $\rho_o$ is the share of organic
records that are duplicated. Treating the repetition count as part of a pair's contents,
this is a lower bound on $\gamma$. Designs that repeat canaries to strengthen memorisation
pay for it before any content is examined: our semantic design repeats three quarters of
its twins, so $\gamma\ge0.75-\rho_o$ with $\rho_o=0.004$ in our organic corpus of $2{,}000$ records,
while single-exposure matched twins pay nothing. Repetition is visible in the corpus, so
the first consequence above, that a hidden manifest leaves only the contents to recognise,
does not extend to it. This is the detectability side of the power
trade-off of Section~\ref{sec:exp-matched}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Organic-matched twins}\label{sec:matched}

The third consequence above suggests a design. Let the organic records be produced by a
generator $G_o$, for example a set of sentence templates with slot distributions, and
draw each canary pair as follows: sample one record $r\sim G_o$, choose one of its slots
(fixed per template), and form the pair by filling that slot with an unordered pair of
values drawn from the slot's distribution and swapped by a fair coin. Each twin is then
a record of $G_o$ with one slot redrawn, so under a slot distribution that is
exchangeable over its values each twin is distributed exactly as an organic record.
We call these \emph{organic-matched twins}. Two details matter. Names are drawn as
in organic data (no distinguishing middle initial), and each canary appears once, since
a record repeated verbatim is itself a signal that organic data rarely gives. The price
is power: a record that looks organic is no easier to memorise than an organic record.

Matching is easy here because we wrote the organic generator. For a deployed model the
organic distribution is the users' own data, and matching it is the hard part of the
problem; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to this.
